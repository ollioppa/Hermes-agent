# Supercycle Thesis

## Trigger

Use for any question about the owner's AI-/energy-/infrastructure-/industrial supercycle, its financing chain, beneficiaries, canaries, or candidate stocks/ETFs/credit vehicles.

## Conditional inputs

Read `docs/research/conditional-inputs.md` first. A thesis-fit verdict requires the operator’s explicitly supplied canonical structural thesis and its claim-level source memo. For credit exposures also require the current regime state, thesis register and relevant entity events. These private cross-project inputs are not distributed. Do not reconstruct them or substitute a generic thesis. Current candidate state comes from this package’s own selected database. For funds inspect methodology, weighted holdings, sector/country/factor exposure, costs, liquidity and overlap.

## Decision discipline

1. Separate technological/industrial success from investor return.
2. Name the actual economic claim owned by the security.
3. Separate the current financier from the eventual cost bearer.
4. Classify links as proven, plausible, or absent; mechanism existence does not establish macro magnitude or timing.
5. Distinguish productive buildout, rent migration, and systemic transmission. For consumer-facing claims, read `docs/research/mechanisms/SKILL.md`: demand architecture can sustain present revenue while binding future purchasing power. Compare technical life, rent life, capital commitments, and household payment duration.
6. Never turn thematic fit into a BUY. Companies follow Business → Debate → Evidence → Price → Decision; ETFs require vehicle-level review.
7. State overlap with the owner's existing thematic exposure before recommending a new position; if holdings are unavailable, label that unresolved.
8. Use one concrete missing condition for WATCH rather than adding layers of gates.

## Output

Lead with one plain-language verdict: direct expression, diluted expression, counter-position, or poor fit. Then explain what the investor actually owns, the strongest thesis link, the largest contaminating exposure, and the evidence that would change the decision.
