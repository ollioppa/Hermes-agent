# Industrial conglomerates with captive finance and listed subsidiaries

Use this recipe only when a manufacturer combines an industrial business, a material captive finance arm, or separately listed controlled subsidiaries.

## Point-in-time sequence

1. **Choose the security before valuing the group.** Record exchange, share class, voting/economic rights, ADR ratio, currency, quote timestamp and source. If ordinary and preferred classes both trade, value each class with its own price and outstanding shares.
2. **Check post-period events.** After reading the latest annual or interim report, search the issuer's ad-hoc and guidance releases through the evidence cutoff. A newer warning or impairment becomes the current forecast authority.
3. **Split the group boundary.** Build separate rows for industrial/automotive operations, captive finance, equity-accounted joint ventures, and controlled listed subsidiaries. State whether each item's revenue, operating profit, debt and cash are consolidated, equity-accounted or outside the group.
4. **Normalize profit conservatively.** Bridge reported to economic earnings item by item. Distinguish cash restructuring, non-cash impairment, working-capital movement, tax timing and management-defined adjustments. Recurring restructuring is an operating burden even when management labels it special.
5. **Use the issuer's industrial cash-flow definition where available.** If none exists, label any reconstruction as a proxy and disclose exclusions. Do not derive industrial FCF from consolidated OCF-minus-capex when finance receivables and funding are material.
6. **Reconcile listed stakes without double counting.** Calculate the parent's economic ownership of each subsidiary at current prices, then show non-controlling interests and whether subsidiary cash/debt sits inside industrial net liquidity. Treat stake value as evidence of a conglomerate discount, not as additive free value.
7. **Test realization, not just arithmetic.** Ask what can close the discount: disposals, buybacks, dividends, deconsolidation, governance change or durable margin repair. If controlling families, government vetoes or labor co-determination can block these paths, make the governance discount part of the thesis rather than a footnote.
8. **Finish with one observable condition.** Prefer an operating or cash-flow proof that includes recurring restructuring costs. A low P/E, high dividend yield or stake-value discount is not itself a promotion condition.

## Reader output

Present in this order:

- investable security and ownership map;
- current guidance, including newer ad-hoc changes;
- industrial versus finance earnings and cash flow;
- strongest earning assets and largest value leaks;
- valuation with explicit anti-double-counting caveat;
- governance and realization path;
- verdict and exactly one missing condition.
