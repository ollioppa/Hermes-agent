## Review discipline

### Platform customer-friction and extraction review

Use this procedure when assessing whether platform profits come from customer utility or from customers overlooking charges, abandoning claims, or being unable to leave:

1. Define the precise disputed mechanism before searching: paid defaults/opt-out extras, hidden compulsory fees, misleading urgency/rankings, cancellation obstruction, refund obstruction, or responsibility shifting. Distinguish `dark patterns` (manipulative choice architecture) from `sludge` (burdensome friction); neither label proves intent in a particular case.
2. Read public terms, fee disclosures and cancellation/refund instructions, then acquire relevant regulator findings and original court decisions. Trace who sets the condition, who collects the fee, whether the platform receives commission when no service is delivered, and who bears the cost of enforcing the customer's rights. Do not credit booking/payment infrastructure as reliable dispute resolution without separate proof.
3. Inspect the public navigation in a browser where useful, including a guest route. Record only demonstrated steps. Without an authorized real reservation, describe this as public-path inspection, not an end-to-end cancellation/refund test; never create a booking merely to test an investment thesis. Missing dynamic content or authentication is a test boundary, not proof of deliberate obstruction.
4. Separate evidence levels: contractual or judicially established mechanism; documented individual harm; representative prevalence; inferred intent; financial materiality. A complaint supports that case, not systematic prevalence. Historical remediation does not prove unchanged current practice; a current adverse judgment does not establish that every group brand or all earnings use the same mechanism. Keep transparent restrictive tariffs and necessary security checks distinct from manufactured friction.
5. Give the personal investment consequence explicitly. State whether the exact alleged mechanism was established and whether other verified customer-treatment evidence independently crosses the owner's exclusion boundary. If the recommendation changes, identify the new evidence or preference and retract the old personal proposal; do not invent a company-wide economic DROP or mutate Radar state. If material evidence predates the original cutoff but was missed, call it a research coverage gap rather than a new event.

- Preserve the purpose of a third-party danger list: when the owner asks whether the stockpicker would have detected or rejected its risks, explain the source's actual threatened profit mechanism and compare it with our review, rather than silently searching for bargains. For each tested issuer give the stockpicker's actual verdict, the observed damage or warning, and your own agreement/disagreement with the danger thesis; then state one overall judgment. Distinguish a thematic trading basket from issuer-specific investment verdicts, and prospective margin/rent-duration damage from disappearance of the business; current growth alone does not falsify a future threat. A positive price-dependent investment proposal is not an all-clear on the mechanism. If testing independent detection, freeze an ordinary stockpicker review without the third-party verdict before comparing it with the source: a reviewer already prompted with the warning cannot prove it would have discovered the risk unaided. Keep this comparison downstream of any explicitly required complete initial scan.
- When explaining a sharp price move around a corporate event, reconstruct the raw and total-return-adjusted daily path before assigning a cause. Mark the ex-dividend date and amount, announcement timestamps, results release and first executable close; compare the same windows with the broad index and phase-matched peers. Separate mechanical distribution adjustment, unexplained pre-event drift and post-announcement repricing. If no material public catalyst explains the drift, say that explicitly rather than backfilling a plausible narrative.
- Treat management change as a cluster audit, not a single headline. Check which economic regions or profit pools each departing executive controlled; planned versus immediate timing; notice and handover; retirement, poaching or dismissal; internal versus external succession; simultaneous regional departures; replacement status; and the successor's operating and capital-allocation record. One orderly retirement may be neutral, while several top-level departures can remove the management-continuity premium even without evidence of a hidden operational break. Do not use unchanged guidance alone to dismiss governance, retention or strategy-reset risk.
- Audit each material successor through the same stakeholder bridge: `customer utility and price → company capability, reinvestment and normalized cash generation → employees/suppliers or platform participants bearing hidden costs → shareholder distributions and net share-count change`. Reconstruct the predecessor role in phases, strip out pandemic, scarcity, acquisition and working-capital windfalls, and compare the opening and closing normalized margin rather than crediting the peak. Separate decisions the executive controlled from group decisions and events that merely occurred during the tenure; when segment profit, ROIC or capital authority is undisclosed, state that personal attribution is unproved. For platform operators, inspect ranking, customer-data use, self-preferencing, commissions, advertising and complaint access because shareholder monetization can externalize cost to dependent sellers without visibly worsening the consumer product. Treat payouts during negative free cash flow or rising leverage as harvested prior rent, not proof of recurring capital-return capacity. After succession, demand customer volume/frequency/share, service economics and continued reinvestment before treating a higher margin or buyback as transformation success.
- For a portfolio-fit question, inspect the current owned-position ledger and relevant off-ledger exposures first, then name the proposed security's job (core, diversification, thematic growth, income, hedge, or speculative satellite). Compare its underlying economic risk with existing holdings, not just its sector label: a different business can still amplify the same liquidity, valuation, or credit-cycle loss. Separate `good company`, `priced investment`, and `useful portfolio role`; a WATCH case is not an owned position or a buy. If the complete household weights are unavailable, give a role judgment without inventing an allocation percentage. Lead with that judgment and only the decisive company evidence; do not turn a portfolio-fit follow-up into another full deep dive.
- When an interactive reassessment changes the personal verdict after a scheduled brief was produced, treat that brief as a dated prior view, not current authority. State the discrepancy explicitly; do not silently repeat its decision-ready language or mutate product state without the required authorization.
- For an apparent dividend or buyback yield, separate recurring from special distributions, paid from merely announced returns, and gross repurchases from net share-count change. Compare recent multi-year distributions with cash after required investment and compensation, not just a long-period aggregate that can hide a recent funding gap.
- Record the user's rejection reason by type instead of flattening every rejection into a negative company call: `circle of competence / predictability`, `portfolio overlap`, `customer-treatment / extraction exclusion`, or `insufficient evidence / revisit trigger`. If the owner excludes monetization through hidden defaults or deliberately obstructed cancellation, refunds, and free options, a cheap valuation does not override that declared personal exclusion. Distinguish his stated boundary from your assessment that evidence meets it, and keep both separate from a forecast of earnings collapse. This preserves why a candidate was rejected and whether later evidence could legitimately reopen it.
- Treat entry, exit and re-entry as separate investment episodes. Never infer that an exit after price stagnation was caused by impatience when the investor may have reassessed product concentration, renewal capacity or compensation for risk. Freeze the actual exit thesis and valuation at that date. Allow a new episode when a previously hypothetical capability becomes industrialized and customer-verifiable; Apple's M1 is the canonical pattern of a system capability moving from concept to a category-changing shipped product, not proof that the earlier concentration-based exit was wrong.
- Assign `WATCH` on an absolute investment basis, never because a company is merely healthier or earlier in the deterioration cycle than weaker peers. Premium positioning, remaining margin cushion, or not-yet-severe external pressure do not prove a moat or positive inflection.
- In technology transitions, distinguish passive breadth from executable optionality. Credit optionality only when several paths are already industrialized or being implemented and the company can reconfigure production, software, and capital without forcing an unproven technology onto legacy structures. Verify current execution before describing transformation as only hypothetical. Map each critical capability as owned, group-shared, partnered, or externally sourced: partnerships can preserve capital and speed entry, but they may also surrender control, differentiation, or rent capture. For a smaller manufacturer, test whether partnership-backed breadth is a durable operating model or merely a necessary response to insufficient standalone scale.
- In commoditizing industries, separate industrial success from shareholder rent capture. Rapid volume growth, international expansion, and technology leadership can still produce mediocre returns when price competition transfers the cost advantage to customers; test whether valuation already assumes dominance plus durable margins and cash generation.
- For fashion businesses, do not present brand, distribution, or customer data as a durable moat without evidence of repeat behavior across cycles; treat current cultural relevance as potentially reversible.
- When the owner asks whether a polarizing social or political campaign damaged a consumer brand, address the *execution and customer-alienation mechanism* directly rather than reframing his concern as opposition to the people represented. Identify and inspect the actual creative, audience, timing and recurring message before judging a remembered campaign; correct its wording without losing the user's branding point. Compare the creative with the brand's prior aspiration or product promise: what does it show versus explain, what can viewers project onto the athlete or product, and does an identity or political message displace the intended emotional payoff? Treat repeated message drift and customer alienation as a plausible leading brand-risk mechanism even without an isolable revenue loss; test polarizing effects across customer segments rather than generalizing one viewer's response to everyone or implying a represented person's identity precludes achievement. Distinguish documented backlash or stated purchase intention from observed behavior, retention, margin or licensing damage. Compare the same period with product quality, channel strategy, competition, geography and secular business-model shifts before assigning stock or sales declines to the campaign; neither dismiss the brand risk as mere noise nor assert a quantified causal loss without an isolating measure. Check whether later marketing repositioning, brand tracking and segment results support a change in brand meaning and persistent damage; a strategic return to core messaging is evidence of repositioning, not proof of the cause or size of prior losses. State separately whether the psychological mechanism is plausible, whether behavior changed, and whether it explains the investment case at the current price.
- When the owner corrects one empirical claim and asks you to search, resolve that exact claim first: locate a primary disclosure and dated offers, compare them with the relevant demand metric, then answer with concrete examples and the remaining causal limit. Do not substitute a conceptual phase explanation, launch an adjacent filmography or repeat the broad investment thesis; the follow-up needs the missing evidence before any wider synthesis. If you promised a bounded list or other concrete deliverable, finish and show it before returning to interpretation; do not silently withdraw the promise after researching it.
- For entertainment/franchise businesses, apply the existing value-creation-versus-extraction lens across the IP chain: original stories and repeat viewing → sequels/remakes/spinoffs → streaming/merchandise → parks and per-guest monetization. A successful sequel, higher box office, or park spend proves current earning power, not renewal of beloved IP; distinguish new enduring characters and voluntary repeat engagement from harvesting inherited affection. Use the six-phase Moat-Liquidation model as qualitative diagnosis, not a score: value creation, earned monetization, structured extraction, strong numbers masking weak renewal, moat erosion, forced reset. Do not infer company-wide erosion from one family's disengagement while attendance and spending still rise. Separate story/acting/casting execution from identity itself, and keep non-Disney productions out of Disney evidence. For parks, read current issuer disclosures and public offers before assigning a Moat-Liquidation phase: promotions, bundled tickets and free add-ons may already be supporting attendance even while visits and spend per guest rise. Call a documented need for promotions a present warning sign rather than arguing it away with the absence of a proven company-wide collapse; compare offer frequency/depth, realized net price, attendance and repeat intent across periods to distinguish seasonal yield management from demand repair. Test whether paid conveniences preserve the family experience or gradually diminish repeat visits; distinguish revenue per guest from durable goodwill. When consumer credit or BNPL enters the thesis, verify actual checkout options and population-level household stress separately, then keep financing (who advances cash now) distinct from demand (who ultimately pays); grocery BNPL users are not automatically the park's customer mix.
- When the user already has thematic ETF exposure, distinguish a portfolio-construction rejection of the direct stock from a negative judgment on the company.
- For an employer's stock, acquire and discuss only public information; do not solicit, infer from, or operationalize non-public employer information. Separate the company-quality verdict from the user's personal investability: account for correlated human-capital exposure and any compliance constraints without treating either as evidence about the business.
- Treat an employee's account of reorgs, resource shifts, process breakdowns or management churn as private context, not empirical proof. Do not endorse its alleged market or financial effect until public numbers have been time-aligned and tested; do not ask for further internal detail.
- When testing an operational-causality claim, first build a dated public scorecard: benchmark-adjusted stock return across the claimed regime, ARR/organic growth where available, revenue-recognition and acquisition/divestiture bridges, operating margin, and operating/free cash flow. State separately whether the data confirms the event, the operating damage, the stock reaction, or none of these; a falling stock or plausible narrative proves neither causation nor organizational failure.
- For employer-stock programs, establish the owner’s explicit compliance, tax, currency and administrative constraints before making a personal ownership recommendation; do not assume private preferences.
- Do not persist an out-of-band research case, install monitoring, or create reminders when the owner chooses ad-hoc follow-up; retain the stated thesis only in the current research response and re-check public facts when he returns.
- Prefer simple systems over stacked filters.
- Do not add workforce culture, employee sentiment, or attrition as a systematic stock-selection metric without new point-in-time evidence of incremental value. Use workforce evidence only as qualitative context or a causal explanation inside an existing deep dive unless a future blind comparison proves genuine lead information.
- Distinguish a component test from a test of the full review process.
- Do not call an exploratory retrospective funnel validated.
- Preserve WATCH/REJECT cases, not just purchases, so selection skill can be measured.
- Freeze each live case before outcome reveal: data cutoff, sources, decision, thesis, probability definition, horizon, counterpath, and falsifier.
- Do not reuse aggregate historical hit rates as a case-level calibration corpus unless the company names, frozen verdicts, cutoffs and source packets are recoverable. Missing provenance is an evidence boundary; never infer or recreate the cases from memory.
- For outcome checks, bind the start to the first genuinely executable market close after the information release, especially for after-hours earnings. Use split/dividend-adjusted returns against the same-start benchmark, and report business-mechanism correctness separately from stock performance; a damaged business can still outperform after repricing.
- Apply the cutoff to each source's publication timestamp, not to the accounting period it describes. When an earnings release or call precedes the 10-K, 20-F or annual report, use only the release, call and older filings in the blind verdict; mark undisclosed cash flow, concentration or segment detail as unavailable instead of backfilling it from the later filing.
- Before freezing a point-in-time verdict, search the issuer's ad-hoc releases and guidance changes published after the latest periodic report. For a customer-treatment or legal-risk thesis, also check regulator, consumer-association and court publications through the cutoff; an issuer-only freshness search can miss an adverse judgment. Read linked original decisions rather than treating a complaint page as the final procedural state. Distinguish allegations, undertakings, judgments, geographic scope and verified finality. A newer profit warning, impairment, capital action or forecast revision overrides the older quarterly narrative; reconstruct the economic bridge before using the periodic figures.
- For a turnaround or disrupted earnings year, bridge reported profit to a plainly labelled economic-normalization before applying P/E, FCF yield, or a margin-recovery thesis: isolate material one-off recoveries, charges, tax/legal items and working-capital distortions, show their direction, and do not silently treat management-adjusted earnings as sustainable cash generation. Verify period semantics against the underlying cash-flow table: reconstruct TTM as latest FY + current YTD − prior-year YTD rather than accepting a first-pass FCF label. If a positive valuation depends on a sharp recent improvement, run the same reverse valuation on the previous completed year's identically normalized cash; this exposes how much of the apparent cheapness depends on continuation of the new run rate.
- For manufacturers with captive finance, leasing or banking arms, separate industrial operating profit, industrial cash flow and industrial net liquidity from consolidated cash flow and finance debt. Never label consolidated OCF-minus-capex as industrial FCF unless the issuer provides or the analysis reconstructs the segment boundary.
- For conglomerates with listed subsidiaries, use sum-of-the-parts only as an ownership and discount diagnostic. Do not add listed-stake market values to consolidated earnings, cash or net liquidity: the subsidiaries are already consolidated and minority interests, pensions, leases and holding-company obligations must be reconciled first. Use `docs/research/stockpicking/references/industrial-conglomerate-valuation.md` when this structure is material.
- Bind every market-price-derived valuation to the exact quote timestamp and source; distinguish reported, normalized and forward valuation rather than mixing their denominators. When multiple share classes or ADRs exist, choose the investable security explicitly and calculate total equity value across all classes rather than multiplying one quoted class by every share.
- Compare BUY with SPY, BUY with WATCH/REJECT, and BUY with a candidate-space control group.
- Treat Black-Swan counterfactual outcomes separately from realized returns and show both.

For vehicle manufacturers facing an electrification transition, use `docs/research/stockpicking/references/automotive-transition-analysis.md` to test executable optionality, battery-system choices, regional exposure, partnership economics, and shareholder rent capture without confusing product success with an investable moat.
