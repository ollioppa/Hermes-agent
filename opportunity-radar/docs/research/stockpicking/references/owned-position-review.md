# Portable reference

Paths in backticks are relative to the package root unless explicitly described as output examples. Read only modules whose triggers apply. Provenance and excluded private inputs: `PROVENANCE.md` and `docs/research/conditional-inputs.md`.

## Owned-position review and exit lifecycle

Keep owned positions separate from Opportunity Radar cases. The Radar answers whether a security merits attention or entry; the position ledger answers whether the owner should still own an actual exposure. Linking the two is useful, but a position review must never mutate `WATCH` or `READY`, and a Radar transition must never imply a trade.

Keep the owner's stockpicker position ledger narrower than a household wealth inventory: exclude direct cryptoassets, crypto ETPs and experimental/test purchases even when they appear in a broker export. Include only deliberately held stocks or in-scope ETFs with an active investment purpose; a crypto-linked listed company such as MSTR remains eligible when the owned security itself has a distinct equity thesis and exit plan.

1. **Reconcile the owned inventory before enriching it.** Assign field-level authority instead of treating one document as authoritative for everything: use broker/portfolio exports for identifier, quantity, average price and cost; use the owner's separate strategy notes only for goal, horizon and exit intent; keep today's analytical reconstruction distinct from both. If the owner says to ignore the numbers in a strategy list, never let its rough size or cost overwrite the quantitative export; retain only numbers intrinsic to the strategy itself, such as a duration, target exposure or exit threshold. Parse locale-formatted CSV values explicitly. Exclude zero-quantity rows from the active ledger unless the owner asks for history, preserve duplicate lots or custody rows until their identity and aggregation semantics are confirmed, and stop on ambiguous mappings rather than joining by similar names. Do not coerce an unsupported instrument such as direct crypto or a commodity claim into `stock` or `etf`; make the smallest honest contract extension at the asset-class owner.
2. **Freeze the smallest honest baseline.** For an older holding without a point-in-time record, start with verified purchase facts plus the owner's remembered `goal`, `horizon` and `exit intent`; explicitly record `not defined` where he has no exit rather than inventing one. Rich carrying claims, priced-future fields, counterpaths, falsifiers and a seven-part exit taxonomy are research enrichments, not mandatory import fields when the user supplied only a simple strategy. Add them later through a reviewed baseline revision, clearly labelled as contemporaneous evidence or today's reconstruction, never as the original thesis. A price decline alone is not a thesis break unless the recorded strategy defined a price, liquidity or financing boundary.
### Reached targets and reinvestment choices

1. Read the owned baseline with `show-position` before interpreting a reached target. Distinguish a mandatory sale, a reassessment trigger, and an estimated fair value; an investment horizon is not a minimum holding period unless explicitly defined.
2. For a target-origin question, recover the original reasoning from the earliest relevant conversation or strategy artifact, not merely its later import into the ledger. Separate `target documented` from `target economically derived`. If derivation is unrecovered, state that boundary and do not reconstruct chart resistance, desired return or valuation assumptions as historical fact.
3. Base a fresh EXIT recommendation on today's forward shareholder economics and strongest hold countercase, not on target attainment plus a plausible normalization narrative alone. Label sector-risk analysis separately from a completed ETF valuation; an aggregate multiple or oil-price forecast does not establish the fund's fair value or quantify its remaining return. Do not describe an unknown original thesis as completed merely because its price marker was reached.
4. For existing reinvestment choices, inspect both Radar cases and the candidate inbox's latest episode-bound decisions. Start with `list`; if the CLI lacks an inventory reader, inspect the schema and query SQLite with `mode=ro`. Read the substantive verdict and open work rather than treating `AWAITING_USER` as proof of decision readiness. Present completed proposals, conditional WATCH cases and unfinished research separately; compare existing core ETFs without implying new ETF research was completed.
5. Before presenting an old proposal as buyable now, bind its reference price and cutoff, reconcile later interactive corrections, and refresh only the decision-relevant evidence. Keep an exit decision separate from choosing its replacement; lack of a replacement is not evidence for holding the old exposure.

3. **Keep preview and confirmed import mechanically safe.** Make preview genuinely read-only, including linked-case lookup: open existing SQLite state with `mode=ro` so a failed dry run cannot create or migrate a database. Before a confirmed write, require non-empty values only for the minimal baseline actually authorized, finite positive numbers for price/quantity/cost, canonical dates, and strict JSON serialization with non-finite values disabled. Normalize the security identifier once and use the committed ID or normalized identifier for readback; otherwise a successful write can be reported as a failure. Insert position and baseline in one transaction and prove a rejected baseline rolls the position back.
3. **Use one monthly portfolio review, not a trading cadence.** Review all active stockpicker positions together once per calendar month. The reader receives one consolidated editorial email with orientation, portfolio context, material changes, action needed, and short position sections. Never drip one position per day or send daily/weekly position messages merely to spread compute. A partial run may resume missing positions but must not send until the monthly set is complete.
4. **Emit one recommendation per position and explicit month/version.** For an opportunity position, ask what is now priced, what shareholder-value path remains from today's price against SPY in EUR, and how much time remains until the original endpoint. An intact old business thesis is not alone a HOLD argument; consumed asymmetry requires a TRIM/EXIT assessment, not an automatic trade. Core and hedge holdings retain their confirmed purpose. Compare elapsed and remaining time, explain delay, and use a confirmed baseline successor for extensions rather than resetting the clock. Bind the review to the baseline actually read; monthly corrections are explicit append-only successors. Each completed review ends in `HOLD`, `TRIM`, `EXIT`, or `NO_DECISION`, with strongest counterevidence, no score. `NO_DECISION` needs a real missing fact and future check date; missing information is not positive HOLD evidence.
5. **Separate recommendation from execution.** Automated research never changes quantity/status. Confirmed trims/exits bind execution reference plus position episode: identical retries return the original event even after EXIT, conflicting payload is rejected. Check known purchase/event chronology before changing holdings; do not fabricate unknown history or silently post ambiguous backdated changes. Preserve actual date, quantity, optional price and reason. Add/re-entry expansion is not authorized by this repair.
6. **Preserve episode history minimally.** Keep the confirmed baseline, reviews, sources and confirmed trim/exit events readable. Represent a baseline correction once as a confirmed superseding baseline version, not again as a duplicate event. A closed position leaves review selection but remains readable; a later re-entry starts a new episode.
7. **Keep delivery reader-led.** Send one monthly email through the existing verified delivery path. Introduce unfamiliar or complex matters in plain language as `what this is → why it matters → what it means in practice` before specialist detail. Do not assume the reader knows LLM, agent, workflow, status, or system vocabulary; internal codes and operational mechanics stay out of the reader product unless needed for a decision.
