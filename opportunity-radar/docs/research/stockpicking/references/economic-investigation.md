## User intent

the owner wants the analysis to remain maximally simple, while accepting that a minimal company review is necessary. Do not turn every discovered relationship into another mechanical gate or an elaborate professional-style scoring model.

## Macro and sector role

Treat macro/sector analysis as context, transmission mechanism, relative benchmark, trigger, and falsifier — not as a standalone alpha selector. A strong sector plus a strong company does not guarantee stock outperformance because expectations may already be priced.

Use the smallest useful macro block:

1. What demand, financing, regulatory, or sector regime matters?
2. How does it transmit into revenue, margins, or cash flow?
3. Is the company developing better or worse than peers/end markets?
4. How much of that path is already priced?
5. What observable development falsifies the thesis?

When a macro thesis is fundamentally about financing rather than operating growth, map the investable claim before selecting stocks: distinguish manager fee equity, insurer spread equity, BDC residual equity, direct debt funds, senior structured credit, and thematic operating equity. Do not jump from “credit demand grows” to a BDC or lender recommendation; compare where the economics accrue, whether the listed security owns the contractual claim, and whether the position adds a new portfolio function or merely repackages existing thematic and liquidity risk.

When explicitly asked to evaluate a company against a particular operator thesis or maintained domain radar, consult the supplied current state, latest report, thesis register and entity-relevant events. Treat those inputs as the authority for that operator-specific comparison; company filings do not substitute for an unknown personal thesis or portfolio context. For ordinary company or ETF research, including alternative managers, insurers, BDCs, CLOs and private-credit vehicles, research the required industry, credit-regime and cross-company evidence from authorized public sources and disclose gaps. Sector membership alone does not require a private radar. Join evidence within the existing review, without adding a gate, score or validator; leave unsupported personal-fit claims open.

Treat non-operating vehicles such as closed-end debt funds and credit ETFs outside the company stock-review funnel. Review their portfolio claims, NAV mechanics, liquidity, access, fees, tax, and currency separately instead of forcing them into a business-quality analysis.

## Reader-led deep dives

Before presenting a verdict, give the reader enough orientation to form an independent view:

1. Explain in plain language what the company sells, who pays it, and what causes revenue and cash flow to rise or fall. Then trace the full value chain in W-questions: who brings the customer, who owns the customer relationship, who supplies the product/infrastructure and capital, where fees and risks accrue, who can bargain away the margin, and what precisely the listed security retains. Answer what this company does differently from named credible peers; profitability alone is not differentiation.
2. Name the actual investment dispute as two competing interpretations, not as a wall of ratios.
3. Explain each specialist metric only at the point where it changes that dispute.
4. Separate a durable structural moat from a temporary brand, fashion, cycle, scarcity, or narrative advantage. Never describe fashion relevance as a hard moat; assess hype breadth, monetization, persistence, and concentration instead.
5. Test rent durability without creating a new score: name the scarcity source, separate replication from substitution, and compare the expected rent duration with both capital payback and the duration embedded in the valuation. Test any claimed technology, licence, integration or distribution edge against at least one named credible peer using primary evidence; separate management's claim, observed operating proof, and demonstrated incremental rent capture. If peers own comparable capabilities or segment margins/customer retention are undisclosed, say the advantage is unproven rather than calling it a moat. A price can support a no-moat earnings case, but never let cheapness silently substitute for proof of differentiation or label the case decision-ready on that untested premise. Treat capital intensity as a mismatch question, never as an automatic quality penalty. Do not count the same substitution claim again as separate moat, capex, and valuation defects.
6. Treat attack and renewal symmetrically. Name the earliest credible replication, substitution, avoidance or regulatory path and the strongest credible counterforce through contracts, qualification, integration, cost leadership, product renewal or demand rebound. A plausible attack is not yet proven company damage.
7. When a value chain is changing, map where the rent can migrate rather than labelling the whole theme good or bad. Distinguish the technology winner from the security that captures the economics, and the asset owner from the operator, financier, supplier, and end customer carrying different durations and risks.
8. Search actively for positive asymmetry with the same evidence discipline used for risks. Test whether an existing owned asset, operating leverage, renewal capability, underestimated rent duration, or several monetization paths over the same installed base can create more shareholder value than the price requires. State what is already observable, how the security captures it, what success the price does not require, and the strongest direct counterevidence. A large market or management initiative alone is not upside evidence.
9. End with what the reader must believe, what would disprove it, and why the current price is or is not sufficient compensation.
10. Before interpreting the latest quarter or current valuation, build a historical spine covering at least five completed fiscal years or one full economic cycle, whichever is more informative. Show revenue or volume, reported and economically normalized operating margin, industrial cash generation and net liquidity, returns on capital, share count, dividends/buybacks and the security's price/valuation path. Use fewer years only when the company or security is younger, and say so explicitly. Identify whether current weakness is cyclical, a reversal from an exceptional peak, or a persistent structural decline. A current quote, latest-quarter bridge and point-in-time valuation cannot by themselves constitute a deep dive.
11. Treat the historical spine as context, not another score. Explain which historical regime is the valid anchor and why; do not average across business-model changes, acquisitions, pandemic distortions or accounting-boundary changes without an explicit bridge. Do not promote a case to `WATCH` as a completed deep dive while this historical classification remains missing.
12. When the basis of competition is migrating — for example from hardware to software/platform, from scarcity to scaled commodity supply, or from product sales to an ecosystem — treat historical share, margins, brand and R&D scale as evidence about the old regime only. `WATCH` requires either observed competitiveness and rent capture in the new layer or a price that already assumes material erosion of the old rent pool; management initiatives and optionality narratives are not proof.
13. Quantify product, customer and capacity concentration before crediting growth. If the issuer does not disclose enough to establish diversification, record the opacity as unresolved rather than assuming it. Concentration is not itself a rejection: distinguish dependence on a replaceable customer, channel or fashion franchise from concentration created by a dominant integrated product or bottleneck whose installed base, qualification burden, switching cost, repeat demand and pricing evidence were already observable at the cutoff. Do not treat end-market growth, patents, order commentary or subsidized capacity as substitutes for diversified customer demand or proven lock-in. Growth capex earns no durable-moat credit until utilization, customer breadth and post-capex cash conversion are visible; peak EBITDA with negative free cash flow is not self-funding proof.
14. Count independent economic dependencies, not projects. Separate monetization paths over one asset from platform renewal, regional recovery and refinancing risks. Put those dependencies into the preferred and damage paths with explicit financing and capital-use assumptions; do not manufacture certainty or a single WATCH condition from several remaining repairs. Apply the canonical mandate once at the decision boundary; this reference supplies no independent pre-inflection gate.

Treat rent durability as a structured debate inside the existing deep dive, not as another mechanical gate. A large end market or a current bottleneck is insufficient unless the company can retain the rent longer than competitors or substitutes need to attack it.

For property-and-casualty insurers, separate accident-year underwriting from calendar-year reported profit: bridge current-accident-year loss ratio, LAE, expense ratio, prior-year reserve development and combined ratio before applying a multiple. Treat favorable reserve development as evidence about reserving quality, not automatically recurring earnings. Compare peers only on aligned definitions and channels; different reserve treatment, state mix, coverage and customer selection can make nominal loss ratios incomparable. Never subtract all cash and investments from market value as surplus cash: bind them against claim reserves, unearned premiums, debt and regulatory capital. Include statutory surplus/RBC disclosure, reinsurance retention, share classes, preferred claims, warrants and granted equity awards in the shareholder bridge.

For food retailers, analyze the economic unit at the local banner and distribution-density level before crediting a group-wide moat. Trace cost savings into customer prices, wages, energy, technology and shareholder cash rather than treating the announced savings total as incremental profit. Separate like-for-like price, volume and mix; distinguish local share gains from margin-funded promotions; reconcile online growth with disclosed profitability and incremental capex. Compare gross buybacks with net share-count decline, strip out disposal-funded returns, and treat leases as operating capital in cash-flow and leverage comparisons. When comparing two grocers, separate operational quality from price: the cleaner operator can still be the weaker investment when the lower-quality peer is priced for modest execution rather than dominance.

When a food-retailer thesis depends on private label, distinguish forced trade-down from structural de-branding with three tests: **breadth** across income groups and banners, **quality migration** into premium/bio/convenience rather than only opening-price tiers, and **persistence** in unit share and repeat behavior after the initial inflation or recession shock. Do not infer affluent adoption from premium sales alone; seek income-segment or cohort evidence. Before inferring the adoption clock from grocery penetration alone, separate category maturity from consumer readiness: test whether shoppers have already learned brandless or platform-led trial in adjacent sectors, because reviews, creators, algorithmic discovery, easy returns and platform trust can compress the grocery adoption curve. Treat social virality as a trust-and-status transmission mechanism, not automatically as a durable moat: trace viral trial → repeat purchase → banner-wide trust, and distinguish one-off products, merchandise drops and FOMO from persistent traffic, repeat behavior and own-brand share across banners. A prestige inversion can remove discount stigma, but only a coherent banner transfers that cultural capital across the retailer's portfolio. Phase-match against the prior recession and inspect branded manufacturers as the counterparty: persistent volume loss plus rising price, promotion, product and marketing support indicates erosion of automatic brand rent, while volume-led growth in genuinely differentiated brands bounds the claim. The likely market shape is a barbell—credible retailer brands and truly differentiated brands squeeze the undifferentiated middle—not the disappearance of all brands. Finally test whether the retailer passes part of the migrated rent to customers or merely replaces the manufacturer rent with shelf, loyalty-data and personalization power.

Treat **value creation versus installed-base extraction** inside that debate. Decompose multi-year revenue and EPS growth into units/new customers/new categories, like-for-like price and mix, monetization of the existing base, operating leverage, and buybacks. Reconstruct three phases against same-phase peers: customer/volume/share quality before an exceptional regime; the source of price, mix and margin during it; and voluntary price normalization, promotions, support intensity plus the resulting volume/share/retention after it. Use positive controls as well as failures: firms that restored affordability after scarcity or cost pressure eased can distinguish durable customer economics from a sector-wide excuse, but examples illustrate a mechanism rather than prove causality.

Separate **where margin came from** from **what management did with it**. Trace whether temporary rent funded customer value, product renewal, productivity, availability or balance-sheet resilience; was returned through lower prices when conditions normalized; or was extracted/distributed without renewing future demand. Compare repeated price-ladder changes with shipped customer utility and behavioral responses such as replacement-cycle length, downgrading to older/refurbished products, promotions, churn, retention, and channel inventory. Rising ASP, margins, services revenue, or EPS are ambiguous when volume and renewal weaken: they may show a healthy moat being monetized or a mature moat being liquidated. Treat pre-regime weakness masked by exceptional pricing as a hypothesis to confirm, reject or leave open—not as a preset verdict. High margins do not prove customer exploitation, and low prices do not prove quality. Identify the phase and causal mechanism rather than manufacturing a scalar score or a new gate. At a premium valuation, `WATCH` requires observable moat renewal or durable customer value, not merely the continued ability to harvest switching costs.

For user-generated-content platforms serving minors, split **generational replacement** from **same-cohort aging and retention**: a long-lived platform can keep acquiring new children without retaining those children as paying adults. Inspect the verification denominator and sampling bias before generalizing reported adult shares; demand cohort-level retention and spending evidence for a lifetime-value claim. Trace parent trust, age checks, moderation, communications restrictions and developer payouts through engagement, bookings, costs and bargaining power rather than relegating child safety to reputational risk. Reconcile cash generated by prepaid virtual currency with deferred service obligations and creator liabilities; neither treat all customer float as free net cash nor subtract its entire face value as immediately repayable debt. Compare gross free cash flow with share-based compensation, actual repurchases and net share-count movement before inferring durable owner cash per share.

The reader-facing order is `business → debate → evidence → price → personal decision frame`, not `verdict → jargon → conditions`. A technically complete analysis that prevents the owner from forming his own view is incomplete.

When the owner asks for the reasons without numbers, answer with the causal mechanism and owned economic claim only. Do not smuggle the ratio table back in as prose; quantitative evidence can follow later if he asks whether the mechanism is actually visible in results.
